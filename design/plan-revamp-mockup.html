The Plan screen, reworked

Plan is the one screen with no way out and two places that answer the same question. The rework gives it a back button, retires the seven weekday rows, and makes the calendar the only place a plan is organised: tap a day, read what was planned and what was actually logged, and set it there — with a repeat rule instead of a permanent weekday slot. Daily, every third day, weekly, every other week. A day holds as many workouts as you plan on it, and the schedule stops being seven timeless pigeonholes and becomes dated rules the calendar can draw.

Settled with Calvin · rev 2
  • The migration stands — one weekly rule per routine, anchored on the Monday of the week it runs, nothing claimed retroactively.
  • No end date, and no until field. A rule runs until it is removed; wait for someone to ask for an end before building one. What that costs is stated in the model section, and it is small.
  • A day can hold several workouts. Which turns out to simplify the model — the per-date override map is gone, replaced by a one-off rule and a skip list.
01

One screen, one organiser

  • ‹ Back, alone in a header row above the H2. Exactly the shape Settings, Insights, Buddy sync and the picker already use (Btn variant="ghost" label={L.back}). Plan is registered in the tabs but has no button in the bar — which until now made it the one screen you leave by guessing. Android's back always worked; nothing on the glass said so.
  • The seven weekday rows are gone. They were the second answer to a question the grid above them already answers, one scroll apart, and they could only express one thing: this weekday, forever. Everything they did is reassigned in section 07 before they go.
  • The day panel is a list now — one row per planned workout, in the order the rules were made. Each row carries the rule it came from (Every week · Wed, Every 3 days) in a quiet accent pill, and that pill is what makes the calendar sufficient on its own: you can see a plan repeating without a second list stating it.
  • One primary Start per day: the first row you haven't logged yet. The others carry a ghost Start ›, so nothing is unreachable and the screen still has a single obvious action. Same rule drives Today's hero (section 07), so the two screens always agree about what "next" means.
  • The dot grammar does not change. Full accent bar = trained, faint bar = planned, nothing = nothing — same widths, same colours, same doneOn-first order that put freeform Saturdays on the calendar. One planned workout and three look identical on the grid; a day's contents are the panel's job, not a fourth dot nobody can read at 15px.
  • August here carries four rules: Chest A every Monday, Back A every Wednesday, Legs A every other Friday from the 7th, and Row 5k every three days from the 3rd — which is what makes today, the 21st and the 24th two-workout days. The faint bars on the 6th and the 9th are Row 5k days that came and went unlogged; that is the diary being honest, not a bug.
  • Back goes to Today, with a floor. router.back() when there is somewhere to go, router.replace('/') otherwise — a notification or deep link can land on Plan cold, and a dead back button is worse than none.
02

Planned above, logged below

  • Two facts, never merged. The rows say what the plan was; the cards under Logged say what happened, read off history as written — the name frozen at log time, vol as totals() counted it, every set in the "70 × 8" format through loggedLine. This half is already built and does not change.
  • Per-row, not per-day. Monday the 3rd had two workouts on it; one got done and one didn't, so one row carries ✓ logged and the other says Planned, not logged. Under the old one-per-day model this day could only have had one verdict, which is precisely what made a rest-day line show up over a logged session.
  • A row is matched to a session by rid, not by position. A logged free session belongs to no row and lands only in the Logged block — which is how Saturday the 8th reads: no planned rows at all, one full card underneath.
  • A past day is a record, so it has no editing. The + Plan a workout line and the row taps show on today and forward only. A rule anchored in the past would re-ring weeks nobody planned, and the diary would grow workouts you never intended to skip.
  • Save as routine stays exactly where it is, on the logged card, with its naming field and its Saved to your routines line. It is the one write this half of the screen does. Two logged sessions still get two cards — that was always true, and it is now matched by the plan above being a list too.
03

Nothing planned is not a rest day

  • Two empty states, and the app can finally tell them apart. Today every day without a routine titles itself Rest day, because absence was the only way to say rest. Now choosing rest is a real act — you skipped a repeat — so a blank day reads Nothing planned and a day you cleared reads Rest day with its own note. Both come free: a skip is stored, so skips[iso] being non-empty with nothing left standing is the chosen rest. No extra field.
  • The skipped row stays on the screen, dashed, with Restore ›. Dashed is the app's "not yet a fact" grammar — the waiting set row, the held CTA — and a cancelled occurrence is exactly that. It also keeps the act reversible in one tap, which a disappeared row would not.
  • Rest is not an option in the sheet any more. With several workouts to a day, "rest" stopped being a thing you pick and became what is left when you remove the rows — so removal lives on the row (section 06) and the routine list is only routines.
  • The empty day keeps L.dayFree — "Nothing planned. Start a free session any time." The line was already right; it just needed a title that agreed with it. The rest day keeps L.restNote, the line Today's hero already shows.
  • Both cards are the same component; what differs is the title, the body line and whether there are rows. No second layout for the empty case, the way the routine list's empty state points at the shelf instead of inventing a screen.
04

Which workout, and how often

  • One sheet, two fields. The routine list is today's ScheduleSheet almost verbatim — every routine, the exercises named underneath, a check on the current pick. What is new is everything below Repeats, and what is gone is the Rest row.
  • It is opened on a date, not a weekday. dayPick: number | null (a day-of-week index) becomes dayPlan: { iso: string, entry?: string } | null — the date being planned, and which existing row is being edited if any. A rule needs an anchor and a weekday has none. Today's week strip hands over an ISO too, so its tap keeps working unchanged.
  • Adding is the default, editing is the same sheet. + Plan a workout opens it empty; tapping a planned row opens it on that row, with the scope question and Remove (section 06).
  • The weekday strip is what the seven rows became. Under a weekly rule the anchor's own day is pre-ticked, and you can tick more: Chest A, every week, Mon and Thu is one rule set once, where the old screen needed two trips through two rows. This is the one thing the retired rows did better, absorbed into the day you were already editing.
  • The sentence under the control is the contract. It states the rule in full, including the anchor date, and it is the same string the row pills and the Routines tab's days line read — repeatLine(repeat, from, L), one formatter, so the three surfaces can never describe a rule differently.
  • Save is explicit here, unlike the old sheet, which committed on the tap that picked a routine. Two fields now depend on each other; committing on the first would write Legs A, once before you reached the part where you said weekly.
05

Three modes, one sentence

  • Only what applies is drawn. Once shows neither the stepper nor the strip. Days shows the stepper only — an interval counted from the anchor has no weekday to pick. Weeks shows both. Stacked here for comparison; in the sheet it is one block that changes shape with the seg.
  • Daily and bi-weekly are values, not options. Every 1 day is daily, every 2 weeks is bi-weekly, every 3 days is the "and so on" — one control instead of a preset list that always turns out to be missing somebody's interval. The stepper clamps at 1 and stops at 12: past that nobody is planning a repeat, they are planning a day, and Once is right there.
  • The controls are the app's own — seg for the unit, Chip for the weekdays. The stepper is the one new primitive, and it is the set row's number gesture reduced to two buttons for a surface where a drag would fight the sheet.
  • The sentence is generated, never a fixed label — and this is where German stops being a translation. English composes uniformly; German switches on the count: Jeden Tag / Alle 3 Tage, Jede Woche / Alle 2 Wochen. Two forms per unit, four strings, chosen by n === 1 — the same problem the fun facts sidestepped by always being plural, met head-on because a repeat of one is the common case.
  • The anchor is always shown. from Mon 17 August is what makes an every-3-days rule readable at all, and it is the difference between a rule and a superstition. It is the date the sheet was opened on; ticking weekdays that exclude it moves the anchor forward to the first day that matches.
  • Weeks are Monday-anchored, like DOW and the calendar grid. Every 2 weeks counts calendar weeks between the anchor's week and the day being tested, so a bi-weekly Mon-and-Sat rule keeps both days in the same week rather than drifting nine days apart.
06

This Wednesday, or every Wednesday

  • The scope chips come first, because they govern everything under them. They only appear when there is something to ask — a repeating row. A one-off row has one reading of any change, so it gets no chips and no question.
  • Just this day never touches the rule. Changing the routine writes a skip for that occurrence plus a new once rule for the replacement — two primitives the model already has, composing into a one-day swap with no third concept. Swapping one Wednesday must not rewrite the twelve after it.
  • Every repeat edits the rule in place. Same anchor, same interval, new routine, and every future Wednesday follows. Past occurrences are not rewritten: the faint bars behind you were the plan you had at the time, and the logged dots over them are what actually happened.
  • Remove is held, and it says which one it means. Every destructive act in this app is a hold — the routine delete, the session ×, Add set — and the label follows the chips: Hold to remove — just this day skips one occurrence, — every repeat deletes the rule. Dashed for the same reason the waiting set row is: not a fact yet.
  • Removing a rule takes its past bars with it, and that is the accepted cost of having no until. The logged dots — what actually happened — are untouched, and they are the part a diary owes you; the faint bars are only ever a record of intent. If that turns out to matter, until is a one-field addition that needs no migration.
  • The sentence spells out both halves — what this day will do, and what the rule still does. Two chips with only a label each is where calendar apps lose people; the app's voice is mechanism first, consequence second, so it just says it.
07

Where the seven rows' jobs went

The weekly rows are the second thing this rework deletes, after Today's chooser. Same rule as that removal: every job the thing did is reassigned before it goes, or the deletion is a regression with a tidier screen.

The rows didNow
Showed all seven days at once, with routine names The calendar, five weeks at once, with a bar per planned day — and Today's week strip for the glance. The names were the rows' one advantage and the day panel gives them back one tap in, which is where you were heading anyway.
Assigned a routine to a weekday The plan sheet, with Weeks · every 1 and that weekday ticked — the identical outcome, plus an anchor date.
Assigned one routine to several weekdays The sheet's weekday strip: one rule, several days ticked. Fewer taps than the rows needed, not more.
Cleared a day back to rest Hold to remove on the row — just this day as a skip, or every repeat to drop the rule. A day with no rows left is rest.
Showed a routine's exercise count per day The sheet's option rows carry it (4 ex, plus the exercises named), and every planned row carries 4 exercises · 14 sets for the day you picked.
Was the second copy of the calendar's answer Deleted. That was the job worth losing.

The shape it all rests on

The model

schedule: Record<number, string> — seven timeless pigeonholes — becomes a list of dated rules and a list of cancelled occurrences. Two primitives, and every state on every screen above is one of them or both. Allowing several workouts a day is what made it this small: the per-date override map from rev 1 existed only to replace a day's single routine, and with a list there is nothing to replace. 

// src/data/plan.ts — pure, like data/stats.ts: dates and rules in, answers out.
export type Repeat =
  | { unit: 'once' }
  | { unit: 'day';  n: number }                      // n=1 daily, 3 = every third day
  | { unit: 'week'; n: number; dows: number[] }      // n=1 weekly, 2 = bi-weekly

export type PlanEntry = {
  id: string
  rid: string          // a routine; rest is the absence of rows, never an entry
  from: string         // ISO anchor — the day the rule was set on
  repeat: Repeat       // `once` is how a single day is planned. No `until`.
}

export type Plan = {
  entries: PlanEntry[]                // insertion order; a day lists them in it
  skips: Record<string, string[]>     // ISO → entry ids cancelled on that date
}

// The whole resolver. Every screen reads the plan through this one function.
export const plannedOn = (p: Plan, iso: string): PlanEntry[] =>
  p.entries.filter((e) => occurs(e, iso) && !p.skips[iso]?.includes(e.id))

// A rest day you chose: something was cancelled, nothing survived it.
export const restChosen = (p: Plan, iso: string) =>
  !!p.skips[iso]?.length && plannedOn(p, iso).length === 0
  • A one-off is a rule, not an exception. { unit: 'once' } — which is why the override map could go: adding a workout to a single day is the same write as adding a weekly one, and a one-day swap is a skip plus a once.
  • Rest is the absence of rows. An entry always names a routine. Storing "rest" as a thing would give it a precedence to reason about against every rule that matched the same day.
  • Nothing is deduped. Two rules can put the same routine on the same day, and the panel shows both rows with their two different pills. Hiding one would mean an entry you cannot see, cannot edit and cannot remove — worse than a day that visibly says you set the same thing twice.
  • Order is insertion order, everywhere. It is what makes "the first unlogged row" a stable answer for both Today's hero and the Plan panel's primary Start.
  • Nothing stores a row per planned day. A year of a weekly routine is one entry; skips are written only when you cancel one. The whole plan stays a few hundred bytes after a decade.
  • The plan starts when you made it. An anchored rule claims no day before its from, which is a real change: today's timeless slots ring every past Monday in the calendar and accuse you of skipping workouts that were scheduled retroactively. After the migration the past keeps its logged dots and loses only the fictional rings.
  • No until, by decision. A rule runs until it is removed, and removing it takes its past faint bars with it. That is the one thing an end date would have bought, it costs no data (the logged dots are what happened), and it is a single optional field to add later with no migration if anyone asks.
  • The migration must read the raw blob. filterPersisted drops every key not in PERSIST, and schedule leaves that list — so migrateV3 takes the raw parsed object the way migrateV1 does, not the filtered one migrateV2 gets. Wire it the other way and every phone silently wakes up with an empty plan.
  • v3 → v4: group the seven slots by rid and write one weekly entry per routine with every weekday it held — {0:'chest', 3:'chest'} becomes one rule with dows: [0, 3]. from is the Monday of the week the migration runs, so nothing is claimed retroactively and every rule is live the same day. skips starts empty; there is nothing in v3 that could have made one.
  • Backups ride the same path. backup.ts stamps STORAGE_VERSION in its envelope and migrates forward on restore, so a file written this week still loads next month — no new work, provided migrateV3 is added to the chain at the load site as well as the restore site.
  • Buddy sync does not carry the plan and still doesn't. schedule was never in a snapshot; what two phones compare is today's routine by content (routineEquals), which reads through the hero and needs no protocol change. No rebuild of both phones for this one.
The rules that hold it together

Decisions

  • The calendar is the only organiser. One place a plan is read and written, so the two can never disagree. That is the whole rework; the back button and the repeat rules are what make it survivable.
  • One resolver, read everywhere. plannedOn(iso) is the only way any screen learns what a day holds — the hero, the strip, the grid, the panel, the Routines card. A second reading of the rules is how a Wednesday ends up planned on one screen and blank on another.
  • One formatter, printed everywhere. repeatLine writes the row pills, the sheet's sentence, the Routines days line and the routine editor's. Three surfaces describing the same rule in three phrasings is the bug this pre-empts.
  • Planned and logged never merge. Separate blocks, separate sources — the plan from the rules, the session from history as written. A day can be one, both or neither, per row, and all of those have to read correctly.
  • A day is a list; "next" is the first unlogged row. One definition, used by Today's hero and by which Plan row gets the primary Start. A second predicate is how the two screens would start disagreeing about what to do next.
  • Still exactly one live session. Several planned workouts on a day does not touch the start guard — any Start tap while s.session is set resurfaces that session. Planning two and running two means finishing the first.
  • Daily and bi-weekly are values, not options. Every 1 day and every 2 weeks, from a seg, a stepper clamped to 1–12, and a weekday strip that only appears for weeks. A preset list always turns out to be missing somebody's interval.
  • The repeat sentence is generated, and German is written not translated. English composes uniformly (every N days); German switches on the count — Jeden Tag / Alle 3 Tage, Jede Woche / Alle 2 Wochen. Two forms per unit, chosen by n === 1, because a repeat of one is the common case.
  • Weeks are Monday-anchored, like DOW and the grid, so a bi-weekly Mon-and-Sat rule keeps both days in the same week instead of drifting.
  • The past is read-only. No adding, editing or removing behind today. A rule anchored backwards would invent skipped workouts, and the diary records what happened.
  • Removal is held, and scoped. Same grammar as every other destructive act here, with the chips deciding whether it is one occurrence or the rule.
  • UI state stays UI state. daySel becomes an ISO string (which also retires the Math.min(daySel, daysInMonth) clamp on month change), and dayPick becomes dayPlan, carrying the date and optionally the row being edited. Neither is in PERSIST, so the version bump is plan alone.
  • Strings to add: Nothing planned, Plan a workout, Restore, Workout, Repeats, Once / Days / Weeks, Every, the four generated repeat forms per language, Applies to, Just this day, Every repeat, the two hold-to-remove labels, logged for the row tick, and the two-clause consequence sentence. German written rather than translated, per the copy audit.

Open — worth a look on a real phone

  • Every-N-days rules on Today's week strip. Seven dots read a weekly rule perfectly and an every-third-day rule as a jumble. It is honest, and it is the one thing here I would want to see on the phone before calling it done.
  • How busy a two-workout day panel feels with a rule pill, a summary and a Start on each row. If it is too dense, the pill is the first thing to move to a second line.
  • The migration's anchor. Monday of the migration week means the current month loses its faint bars on the days before it. Called neat and kept, noted here only so the first launch after the update isn't a surprise.
