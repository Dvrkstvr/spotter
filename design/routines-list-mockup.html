The Routines tab, reworked

Today the tab is a flat list in the order things happened to arrive. The rework gives it search, an order control and a filter row — and puts the full seed collection on the same screen, grouped the way onboarding already thinks: the routines recommended for how you train first, then the other families. Search reads the library, not just the titles — back finds every routine that trains the back. And with the tab now the one place that starts anything, Today's "choose a workout" row retires, which pays for a lighter Today.

Search · names, exercises, muscles→ Yours · ordered & filtered→ Full collection · recommended first→ + adds a copy to yours
01

Yours, in an order you chose

  • Search sits above everything and reads more than titles — names, the exercises inside, and their muscle groups, in both languages. Section 02 shows it working; the field is the Library's Input, same placement, same grammar.
  • Week is the default order: scheduled routines from today forward (it's Wednesday, so Back A leads and Monday's Chest A files last), unscheduled ones after, in the order they were made. The list's job is "what am I starting?", and the schedule already answers it.
  • Free session moves in from Today's chooser — a dashed row under yours, above the shelf: present wherever starting happens, quiet enough to never compete with a card. Dashed is the app's "not yet a fact" grammar (the waiting set row, the held CTA), which is exactly what an empty session is.
  • Recent is last-trained first, read from history by rid — never-trained sinks to the bottom. A–Z is the name in the active language, resolved through rInfo.
  • The seg only reads; nothing is rearranged in the store. There is no manual drag order to protect — today's "order" is an accident of insertion, which is the thing being replaced.
  • The filter chips are the three style families plus Yours. A routine's family is derived, never tagged: the dominant styleOf across its items — the same fact onboarding reads through routineStyleScore — so saved-as-routine and buddy-built routines file themselves. Yours is anything whose id isn't a seed's.
  • Sort and filter are session state, not persisted — the same decision as the Library's filter and the settings folds (Fold).
  • The cards keep today's grammar — name, days, summary, Start › — one line tighter. Today's day marks itself; the rest of the row stays quiet.
02

Search reads the library, not the titles

  • A match is a name, an exercise inside, or a muscle group — resolved through rInfo / exInfo / gInfo across every language a thing is named in, the same way the Library's search already matches group labels. back and Rücken find the same rows.
  • That's why Legs B turns up: nothing in its title says back, but the Deadlift files under Lower back. The point of searching by muscle is surfacing exactly the routine whose name wouldn't have told you.
  • A found row says why it was found. While a query is live, the meta line leads with the matching exercise and its group — Deadlift · Lower back — because a result that can't explain itself reads as a bug. A row matched on its own name keeps its ordinary counts.
  • Search composes with the chips (intersection) and spans both halves, like the filter. Groups with no matches drop out whole — the Library hides empty groups the same way — and the free-session row sits out: it has no name to match.
  • The sort seg steps aside while a query is live; results order by where the match is (name first, then exercise, then group), which is relevance without inventing a score.
03

The collection, grouped the way onboarding thinks

  • The recommended group is onboarding's pick list, verbatim: routineInStyle filters, routineStyleScore orders — so Back A appears here (its pull-up makes it a little calisthenics) but ranks under the all-strength days, exactly as it does in the tour. One ordering, computed in one place, shown in two rooms.
  • The heading names the style — Recommended · Strength training — so the group explains itself, and re-running setup with a new answer visibly regroups the shelf. Under mixed there is no recommended group at all: "don't sort it" is the option's whole promise, so the three families stand in catalogue order.
  • The other groups are the remaining seeds by their own family — whatever the recommended group claimed doesn't repeat below. Groups fold like the settings lists, recommended open by default, the rest closed (Fold, session-local).
  • + installs what onboarding would have installed: a fresh copy of the seed, level-scaled, non-regular numbers marked planned — the same literal applyOnboarding writes, so an added routine is indistinguishable from a picked one.
  • A seed already on your list shows on your list instead of +, and tapping the row jumps to its card above. Removing stays where it is — the editor's held delete — and the row here simply returns to +. Re-adding hands back a fresh scaled copy, which is what "run setup again" already means for an unticked seed.
  • The collection can never shrink or grow: it renders DEFAULT_ROUTINES straight from code, names resolved per language like every seed. Nothing here is persisted.
04

One filter, both halves

  • The chip narrows yours and the collection in the same stroke. A filter that only thinned the top half would leave the shelf below contradicting it; filtering to Cardio and being shown three strength days to add is the screen arguing with itself.
  • Under a family chip the recommended group steps aside — the question changed from "what suits how I train?" to "what cardio is there?", and the honest answer is the family, whole, in catalogue order.
  • The count line says what the filter is doing — 1 of 5 — so a short list reads as filtered, not as loss. Same reason the Insights footer discloses looseSets: a narrowed view says so, or it lies.
  • Yours filters to non-seed ids and hides the collection entirely — every row down there is by definition not yours.
  • The filter never touches what a chip can't see: scheduling, history and the session are indifferent to it. It is a lens on this screen and nothing else.
05

Empty means the shelf, not a sentence

  • With no routines of yours, the collection is the screen. The current noRoutines paragraph — build one, or rescue a logged day from the Plan tab — retires; a shelf of things to take beats an explanation of where things could come from.
  • This phone answered calisthenics at onboarding and skipped the pick step — the recommended group carries the fringe cases the score keeps honest: Full Body A (one plank in five lines) and Back A (one pull-up in four) appear, but rank under the all-bodyweight days. The strength family below holds only what recommendation didn't claim, so its count reads 4, not 6.
  • Sort and filter controls don't render on an empty list — a seg ordering nothing is furniture.
  • One line of copy does the pointing, in the register the app already speaks: mechanism first, no exclamation mark, + New named exactly as the button is.
06

Today, with the chooser gone

  • The "or start ›" row and its sheet retire together. PickWorkoutSheet listed every routine plus Free session — the reworked Routines tab, one tab-tap away, does the same job with search, order and the collection behind it. pickWorkout was never persisted, so the key and the sheet simply go.
  • The sheet's build-together row moves to the Routines tab, beside Free session, gated on the live buddy link exactly as before — building a routine together starts where routines live.
  • The hero is untouched in essence: done-today badge, last-done line, plan-sync with the buddy, the back-to-session guard on Start. It was already the screen's point; now it's more alone in being it.
  • Seven week rows become one strip. The rows duplicated Plan's list one screen away from Plan. The strip keeps the glance — full dot trained, ring planned, dash rest, today framed — in the calendar's own dot grammar, and keeps the tap (the same day-picker sheet). What it gives back is most of the screen.
  • Recently is new: the last two history entries — name frozen at log time, volume as totals() wrote it — each tapping through to its day on Plan. The diary tab finally shows a little diary.
  • On a rest day the hero's Start gives way to a ghost Free session › — the current rest-day note points at the chooser row this redesign removes, so the way out moves into the card that names the rest.
The rules that hold it together

Decisions

  • One ordering, computed in one place. The recommended group is onboarding's list — routineInStyle + routineStyleScore over DEFAULT_ROUTINES — factored out of the overlay so the tour and the tab can never disagree about what "recommended" means.
  • Family is derived, never stored. Dominant styleOf over a routine's items, computed for seeds and user routines alike. No new field on Routine, nothing to migrate, and a routine edited toward cardio drifts into the cardio chip by itself.
  • Adding is applyOnboarding's move for one routine — fresh copy, level-scaled, planned marks, same-id replace. An added seed must be indistinguishable from a picked one, or it reaches a buddy as a different shape.
  • Sort, filter and the query are UI state. In the store beside filter, outside PERSIST — a fresh launch opens on Week · All, empty search. No STORAGE_VERSION bump anywhere in this feature.
  • Search matches what the screen can explain. Routine names, exercise names and group labels, all languages, through the store's resolvers — never raw .names/.labels. The why-line quotes the match, so nothing turns up that the row can't account for.
  • Retiring the chooser deletes, it doesn't strand. pickWorkout and PickWorkoutSheet go; Free session and build-together land on the Routines tab; the rest-day pointer moves into the hero. Every job the sheet had is reassigned before the sheet goes.
  • The collection reads, the editor writes. No delete, no rename, no reorder down on the shelf; a seed's row flips between + and on your list and that is its entire state space.
  • Strings to add: the three sort labels, the family chips, Full collection, its one-line sub, Recommended · {style} (reusing the onboarding style names), on your list, and the empty-state line — German written, not translated, per the copy audit.

Settled while building

  • The week strip's tap edits — dayPick, the same schedule sheet the seven rows opened. Calvin's call, and the heading already promises it: "Weekly plan · tap to change".
  • Today's day label keeps its accent on the Routines card, and Recently keeps its rows — both earn their place once there is history to draw.
  • "Yours" stayed a chip. A tag on the card would say which routines are custom; only a chip lets you ask to see just those.
  • The recommended group re-ranks live — it is computed at render from s.style, so re-running setup regroups the shelf with no event to fire.
  • The why-line names the group only when the group matched. Found while testing: "Back Squat" answers back by its own name, and the first draft still printed "— Quads" beside it, which reads as though Quads had matched.
  • The first shelf group opens, the rest stay folded. Unfolding all of them on an empty list was a screen and a half of rows; a live search or filter holds every surviving group open, because results hidden behind a fold read as no results.
