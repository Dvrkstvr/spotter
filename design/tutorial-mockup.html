Spotter · mockup

Teaching a gesture where it happens

Half of what this app can do is invisible. A kg cell is a text field that is also a slider; a set number is a button; the bottom CTA sometimes has to be held; the ghost column is tappable; and a chip that reads like a page number is the door to the whole workout. The tour already knows this — its second screen is literally "Four things you wouldn't guess by looking at a screen" — but it runs at minute zero, before the user has ever opened a session, and the door back into it is commented out (see settings-overlay.tsx, the hidden danger corner: finishing the tour re-applies routine picks, so it was taken away). So the one place the drag is explained is a screen you see once, ninety seconds after installing, and never again.

This proposes the other half: a tip fires in the place the feature lives, the first time you are standing in front of it — and retires itself the moment you do the thing. Which is the app's own name as a design rule. A spotter tells you one thing, while you are under the bar, and then shuts up.

The shape of it, in one line each
  • In place, not in a list. The tip renders in normal flow next to the control it is about, so it scrolls with it — no measuring, no re-anchoring, no overlay layer to keep in sync with a keyboard.
  • Never modal. No scrim, no "Next", no step counter. Everything under it stays pressable, because the phone is on a bench between sets and the app does not get to hold it hostage.
  • Doing the thing is the dismissal. A tip that has been obeyed is retired silently; the × is for "I know this already". There is no Got it button to press to get back to your workout.
  • A gesture gets a demonstration, not a paragraph. Words can teach a tap. They cannot teach hold-then-drag, so that one tip performs the gesture on the real cell — with a pointer drawn over it, writing nothing.
  • One at a time, three showings, then never. A tip that is being ignored is furniture, and furniture is what this whole screen is trying not to become.

Why not
Three reasons, and the third is the one that makes it unbuildable here rather than merely unpleasant.
  • It takes the app away to describe the app. You cannot try the gesture while being told about it — the scrim eats the touch. So the user reads it, taps Next four times, and is returned to a screen they now have to remember. Which is the rundown again, one screen later.
  • Four in a row is a tour, and a tour has to be finished. Pips and a Next button turn a hint into an obligation, mid-workout. Anything with a step counter is going to be Skipped in one tap by exactly the person who needed it.
  • The hole cannot stay over the control. A cutout is absolute pixels; the thing it points at moves. The set list scrolls, the live box slides as sets complete, and the keyboard shifts everything through KeyboardAvoidingView a beat after Android reports it — with no will-show event to re-measure from. Every one of those leaves an accent-ringed hole over the wrong thing.
  • It also cannot be quiet. A full-screen scrim in this design system is wash.scrim(66) — the same weight the app spends on a sheet. Spending it on a hint means a hint that looks exactly as important as Restore this backup.
The one thing worth keeping from it
  • Pointing at the control is right. What is wrong is the scrim, the sequence and the modality — so the tip keeps the anchoring and drops the rest: it sits under the thing, in flow, one at a time, with the app still live.

  • The demo shows the real numbers. 0.5 kg per notch, roughly four notches over the pointer's travel, because that is what PX_PER_STEP = 12 and step actually do. A demo that scrubbed 60 → 100 in one sweep would teach a gesture the app does not have.
  • And the real hold. HOLD_MS is 120 ms — a stillness test, not a wait — so the pointer barely pauses before it moves. Drawing a long, deliberate press would teach people to hold for a second and then wonder why the list scrolled out from under them.
  • The ring at the grab is the haptic, drawn. buzz.grab fires when the cell is taken, at the same weight as a ticked set; the demo's ring is that moment made visible, so the buzz is expected rather than surprising. The demo itself buzzes nothing — nobody's phone should vibrate because a hint played.
  • The tip never appears while the keyboard is open. Someone typing has already chosen; interrupting to recommend the other way is the app arguing with a decision it just watched being made. It waits for the next visit.
  • It sits under the live box, not over anything. That is what keeps the tick box, the fields and the CTA exactly where they were — the tip costs one row of the set list, which on the first exercise is empty rows anyway.

  • The Finish line is not a tip, and that distinction is the point of this section. A tip teaches something invisible and then goes away forever. This is a permanent statement of what a button is about to do, shown exactly while it is true (tot.done === 0) — the same fact the summary already reports afterwards as "Nothing was ticked off — nothing logged.", moved to before the press, where somebody hunting for a way out can act on it.   • It reuses the summary's own wording deliberately. savedEmpty and this line are one sentence in two tenses; two different phrasings of "nothing happened" would read as two different rules.
  • No tip fires inside the sheet. By §7's admission test — could a careful person use this for a month and never find it? — everything in here fails the test in the right direction: the rows are named, the counts are numbers, and both buttons carry labels. The one thing to teach was the chip.
  • Adding an exercise needed no tip of its own for the same reason. + Add exercise is a labelled button; it is only unfindable while the sheet is. That is what makes this one tip rather than three — and why it is the chip that gets taught rather than any of the errands behind it.
Two things this drew out that are not about tips
  • The held × on an overview row has no tell. It is a HoldBtn without dashed, so it breaks the app's own grammar — a bordered control that needs a hold says so by going dashed (HoldBtn, the waiting set row, the coach's routine card). A tap on it does nothing and nothing explains why. The fix is the glyph, not a hint about the glyph; noted here rather than papered over with a tenth tip.
  • The Overview docstring still says "Discard". session-overlay.tsx:722 — "Add exercise, Discard and Finish live here too". Discard was removed and Finish took its job; the comment did not follow. Worth a one-line fix while this area is open.

6 · When it fires, and when it stops

The whole state machine, and it is small on purpose. Everything in it is derivable from state the app already keeps, plus one durable counter per tip.

1 · Armed
The precondition holds
You are on the surface, the control is on screen, and the tip's own test passes — for the drag, "first session, nothing logged yet, keyboard closed".
→
2 · Shown
On screen, in flow
Enters on motion.move. It only counts as shown after two seconds — a tip that flashed past while you were scrolling to the next exercise was never read.
→
3a · Retired — performed
You did the thing
The best exit and the common one. Vanishes on the spot, no acknowledgement, no "nice one". done: true.
→
3b · Retired — dismissed
You tapped the ×
Means "I know this", and is believed. Same durable result as performing it.
→
3c · Expired
Shown three times, ignored
It has had its say. A fourth showing is not teaching, it is nagging — and the fourth is the one that becomes furniture.
  • One at a time, and never two on one screen. pickTip returns at most one id, so this is structural rather than a rule each screen has to remember: a surface offers what it could show and is handed back what it should.
  • And one per visit. Most of the session's tips are armed by the same empty live row, so without this a first workout would be a slideshow of eight hints as each retired. The session picks once per mounting and holds it: when that tip is dealt with, nothing replaces it until you next open the screen. Minimising and coming back is a new visit, which is about the right pace — one thing learned per time you look at it.
  • A tip never counts during the tour. The tour covers the whole screen, so a tip mounted behind it would sit out its dwell unread; the check lives in <Tip> itself, since it is the one rule that belongs to no surface.
  • Nothing new is offered over an open keyboard — but a tip already on screen stays. The gate is on the pick rather than the arming, deliberately: unmounting and re-mounting it would be a second showing charged against a budget of three, for a hint nobody looked away from.
  • The trigger is arrival at the moment, not app launch. The ghost tip cannot fire until there is a ghost to tap, which is the second session at the earliest. The hold tip waits for the CTA to actually be dashed. A tip that fires where its feature is not yet usable teaches nothing and burns a showing.
  • Nothing here blocks or gates. Every precondition is a read. If a tip's moment never comes, the feature is simply never explained — which is exactly today's behaviour, so the floor is "no worse".

Mechanics — as built
  • One new pure module, src/data/tips.ts. Like plan.ts and stats.ts: the TipId union, TIP_ORDER (priority), TIP_SHAPE, and pickTip(tips, armed) returning the one tip to draw or null. No store, no hooks, no colours, no strings — which also keeps the React Compiler out of it. What is not in there is the arming: whether a set is open or a rest is running is the screen's own state, and a copy of it over here would be a second reading of the session kept in step by hand. So a surface offers the ids it could show and this answers with the one it should.
  • One new durable key: tips: Record<string, { seen: number; done: boolean }>. PERSIST is additive, so this costs no STORAGE_VERSION bump and no migration over real training data — the same allowance lastMarks, coach and buddySecrets were added under. PERSIST_SHAPE gets tips: 'object'; the rows are the mutator's business, like every other object key that is not the plan.
  • Three mutators and nothing else: tipShown(id) after the two-second dwell, tipDone(id) from the × and from every place that counts as performing it, and resetTips() behind the Settings row. All one-line patches, and tipDone being idempotent means every call site fires it blindly — including NumCell's onUpdate, which runs on every step of every drag forever. patch returns the identical state object when its updater returns null, so React bails out of the render and the repeat calls cost nothing at all.
  • It rides existing events, it does not add listeners. tipDone('drag') goes in NumCell's onUpdate beside the step it already emits; tipDone('tick') in the tick handler; tipDone('mark') where a mark is written. Nothing new watches anything.
  • The demo is drawn, never dispatched. A pointer and a figure on an absolutely-positioned overlay inside the live box — pointerEvents: 'none', no synthetic touches, no writes to the store, and the TextInput underneath stays focusable throughout. A demo that actually drove the gesture would have to write to the set and then take it back, and an app that types into your workout to show you a feature is an app that lost a set once.
  • Motion is by the ladder. The tip enters and leaves on motion.move, the demo pointer's loop is one new linger entry (linger.demo, the length of one taught gesture), and nothing here gets payoff — that is the seal's, once per workout.
  • No haptics. buzz.grab is a report that your finger took the cell; firing it because a picture of a finger did would make the buzz mean two different things.
  • It never leaves the phone. tips is not in any buddy snapshot — what one phone has been taught is not the buddy's business, the same rule set marks follow. A backup carries it (a backup is the durable slice) and mergePersisted ignores it by construction, since it returns only the keys sessions / library / plan touch.
  • The Settings row is not in the danger corner. "Show tips again" writes tips: {} and nothing else — no routine picks re-applied, nothing overwritten — which is exactly why the tour's own row had to be hidden and this one does not.
  • Nothing native, nothing new in the tree. No package, no prebuild, no APK on either phone beyond the usual JS. Expo Go is unaffected.
Where the build differs from the pages above
  • swipe shipped as a Nudge, not a Demo. A pointer arcing sideways would have to be drawn across the set list — over the rows you are being taught to leave — and the gesture is one word anyway. The drag keeps the only demo in the app, which is what "reserved for gestures words cannot teach" was supposed to mean.
  • No tip gates on how many sessions you have logged. The pages above wrote conditions like "first session" and "third session"; the build arms on the situation alone. On a phone that has been training for months every tip is unmet — tips is simply absent from its blob — and a "first session" gate would have hidden the whole feature from exactly the person who has been missing the drag for a year. The seen-counts already say "first time".
  • stats has no gesture to retire it, so it leaves by its × or by expiring. That is the honest shape of a card tip about a screen that only reads: there is nothing on Insights to do.
Known costs, taken deliberately
  • One more thing on the busiest screen in the app. The session view already carries a progress bar, a position chip, a ghost column, a rest countdown and sometimes a buddy's name. The three-showing cap and retire-on-use are the whole defence, and they are the part to hold the line on if this ever grows a tenth tip.
