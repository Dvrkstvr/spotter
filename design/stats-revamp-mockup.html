Insights, rebuilt on fractional sets

The screen that replaces the radar. Same data source — history, re-read — but a different unit and a different question. Today it reports each region's share of mass-weighted volume load, which can only ever say relatively less. This reports fractional hard sets per muscle per week against the range the literature actually states, which can say not enough. See design/stats-research.md for why, and design/muscle-contribution-spec.md for the also field every number on this screen depends on.

01

One card carries the screen

  • The bar is read against the band, not against the other bars. The 10–20 zone is drawn into a fixed 0–24 track, identical on every row, so "am I doing enough of this" is answered without arithmetic and two rows are comparable by eye. The old radar could only say which axis was longest.
  • Under-range is a dimmer fill, never warn. A neglected calf is not a refusal, and warn's meaning is fixed at three sites. accent700 under the band, accent400 in it — the same one-step-down grammar the diary uses for a superset's rule.
  • A region opens to its muscles, and that is where the band is honest. Legs at 12.0/week is not "in range" — it is five muscles, three of them starving under one number. So a region row carries its own rate plus a 3 low count, and the verdict lives one level down. This is the answer to §8's open question in the research doc.
  • Two tags, and the difference is which row is wrong. A region always counts its low muscles, quietly — 2 low — because a region cannot itself be under a per-muscle band. Only a muscle row carries the accent low. Core shows both a dim fill and 2 low: its own rate is short and both muscles under it are.
  • It scrolls, and always did. The second frame is the same screen further down — everything below push : pull survives this rebuild unchanged except key lifts. The card at the top is what grew; nothing else had to move.
  • Nothing is normalised. No REGION_MASS, no bodyKg, no HOLD_SECONDS_PER_REP. Counting sets never introduced the distortion those existed to undo, so the screen stops needing to know anything about the person.
  • The fractional line is a permanent statement, not a tip. It says what the numbers mean and never retires — finishLogsNothing's shape. Without it a 16.5 on Chest looks like a set count that is simply wrong.
  • Needs work names muscles, not regions, sorted by how far short they are — an actionable list, where "Legs 9%" was a comparison with your own bench press. It shows three and counts the rest: eight rows of shortfall is a screen telling you that you have failed.
  • Push : pull is the one ratio a diary can honestly compute — 1:1 is the common default, 1:2 pull-favoured for shoulders. It also says what the six-region roll-up structurally cannot, because Arms swallows both halves.
  • Key lifts moved to estimated 1RM (Epley off the best set). Top weight cannot tell 100×8 from 100×3, so a real session of progress reported "no change".
  • Volume per week stays, unchanged, and stays load-only. Tonnage is a bad cross-muscle comparator and a perfectly good within-diary trend line — this is the one place it belongs.
02

Anatomy of the row

Everything the screen claims is in one 9-pixel track. It has to carry a rate, a target and a verdict without a legend, and it has to do it four to seventeen times down a phone.

The four states

In range 15.5
Under 4.0low
Over 26over
Not trained —

Not trained is not the same as low, and this is the state the muscle-level band forces the app to invent. Nobody trains Neck, and a screen that flags it every week alongside a genuinely neglected calf is noise. A muscle with zero sets in the window shows a dash and stays out of Needs work; it earns a verdict once it has been trained at all. The same rule keeps a group somebody invented from accusing them of neglecting it.

Where the numbers come from

RowReads
a muscle's rateΣ contribOf(ex)[group] over ticked sets, ÷ weeks in the window
a region's rateΣ per set of max over that region's muscles, ÷ weeks — never the sum of the rows under it
the band10–20, fixed. Stated per muscle, which is why the verdict lives at muscle level
push : pullChest + Shoulders + Triceps against Back + Lats + Traps + Biceps, fractional both sides
key liftsEpley w × (1 + r/30) off each session's best load set
volumeeach entry's own vol, untouched — the one figure not re-derived
loose setssets whose contributions reached no region at all

“Arms is pulling behind — 9% against Back's 31%.”

Both halves of that sentence are artefacts. Arms is starved because no press or row credits it a thing, and Back is enormous because the deadlift files under LowerBack and lands there whole. The advice it leads to — do more curls, do less back — is advice about the data model.

04

The headline follows the unit

  • The card keeps its shape, its gradient and its place under the profile header. Only headlineOf changes — and it now has something to say that isn't a comparison with the rest of your own training.
  • The mini bars carry the band too. Four rows at 6px, same geometry as the screen behind them, so the glance and the detail are the same drawing at two sizes. Without the band they would be six bars of nothing.
  • The footer counts muscles under range rather than naming a percentage. It is the one number that decides whether the tap is worth taking.
  • Under MIN_SESSIONS nothing here changes — the card still stands and still makes no claims, because it is the door to Insights and a door that refuses to be opened is worse than an empty one.
