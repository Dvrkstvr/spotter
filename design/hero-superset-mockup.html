Today · the hero card

A pair, at a glance

A routine can join two exercises — with: 'next' — and every surface that draws a routine says so: the editor draws a hairline in the gutter and the word, the overview sheet and the diary hang a rule down the side of the two blocks, the coach's preview borrows the editor's mark. Today's hero card says nothing. It lists tr.items flat, so the one thing about this afternoon's Push day you would actually plan around — that two of those five are taken back to back — is invisible until you have already started it.

What the card can spend
  • The lines are a glance, not a list: 12.5px, 3px apart, a name flexing against a scheme in tabular figures. Five of them read in about a second.
  • The card is above the fold on the app's first screen, over the week strip and Recently. Vertical space here is the most expensive in the app.
  • Whatever is drawn has to work in the live branch too — same lines, 3 / 4 counts in place of the scheme.
  • A badge beside one name is already ruled out: the coach's preview turned that down, because a pair is a fact about two rows and a badge reads as a property of one.

Six ways to say it

Each drawn on the same card, same routine, same pair. What separates them is what they spend — a line of height, a column of width, or the alignment of the names that are not paired at all.

A · Bracket, inset
The diary's own mark — a hairline down the side of the two lines at accent800, one step below the live session's. Costs no height at all. What it costs is width: the two paired names sit 10px in, so they no longer start where the other three do, and the eye reads that as a sub-list before it reads it as a bracket.

Why B rather than C

The argument
  • The word is already carried on both surfaces you meet a pair through. You made it in the editor, where the gutter says SUPERSET; you will stand in it in the overview sheet, which says it again. The hero is neither — it is the glance in between, and it needs to mark the join rather than teach it.
  • Height is this card's scarcest resource and the rule costs none. 14px per pair, on the card above the week strip and Recently on the app's first screen, buys a word two other screens have already said.
  • The rule is the same object at every scale — accent700 in the live overview, accent800 in the diary where it is history. Hung in the margin it takes the brighter of the two, and not as a preference: out there it sits on the gradient's own accent900 corner, where accent800 is a 1px line against a colour four hex digits from it and simply does not draw. A drawn at accent800 works because it is 10px further in, on ground the gradient has already lightened.
  • Nothing moves. Hanging it in the card's own padding is the whole of what separates B from A: five names keep one left edge, and the mark reads as a bracket instead of as two rows that got indented.
What is not being proposed
  • Not F as well. A count in the summary and a bracket on the lines is two readings of one fact, and the count is the half that would go stale.
  • Not a tap. The pairing control lives in the editor; this card is a glance, not a second place to edit a routine.
  • No new copy. B needs no string at all. C would reuse L.superset / Supersatz, which exists — so the choice between them is a layout decision, not a translation one.

What it would touch. todayRoutine.lines and liveLines stop being flat arrays and become groups — the shape stopsOf in data/superset.ts already hands back, which is where the reading should come from rather than a fourth hand-rolled walk over with. Everything else on the card is untouched: the summary still counts exercises and sets, because a pair is still two exercises.

The one thing drawing it found. The bracket's colour is not a free choice out in the margin. Hung at -10 it lands on the darkest part of the hero gradient, and accent800 — the diary's weight — disappeared there completely on the first pass; every frame above was drawn with an invisible pair until it was raised to accent700. Inset (A) it does not need raising, because 10px further in the gradient has already lightened under it. If B is built, that is a value the site owes a comment.

One detail left open. Whether the rule spans only the two lines or bleeds slightly past them, as logPairRule does with its marginTop: 14 to clear the diary's tag. With no tag there is nothing to clear, so it spans the pair exactly — drawn that way above.
